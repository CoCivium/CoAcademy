CoSites + CoNodeMesh research / 24 November 2026

A name is not a site. A site is not a node.

A single CoCivium undertaking can have multiple public names, human and machine entrypoints, source repositories and independent service cells. They need different identifiers, authority, versions and evidence. A domain suffix does none of that work automatically.

The relationships first

Six layers worth keeping distinct

1 / Purpose and governance

Project

CoPolitic remains one project even if its hostname changes.

2 / An address, not the service

Domain

copolitic.org currently returns 404, despite a live research fallback.

3 / A published view of source

CoSite

Human and AI/IA readers may use different presentations of the same evidence.

4 / One exact accessible resource

Endpoint

Press page, JSON, API, or source file. Access does not authorize action.

5 / Service cell or participant

CoNode

May store or serve many sites; may exist offline without a public domain.

6 / What confirms the relationship

Evidence

Version, HTTPS readback, rights, independent receiver, and tested recovery.

The missing edge: The branded CoPolitic hostname has not been proven bound to its working public site. A conceptual relation is not a deployed DNS/TLS redirect.

CoStonk: costonk.org and costonk.com both served GoDaddy parking pages in the 06:36 UTC audit. A future .com-to-.org redirect is only a proposal, not evidence of an investment service, a canonical publication, or domain-ownership authority. Read the dated 36-domain status.

CoNode: A node could be a home server, service process, independent host, offline archive or community-operated cell. That is a capability and custody relationship, not a new TLD or automatic public service. Examine the existing federated-cell contract.

Actual DNS versus naming imagination

What the suffix establishes

The IANA public TLD list updated on 23 November 2026 includes literal COM, ORG, NET and SITE, but not literal NODE. Its XN--NODE line is a different internationalized label, not the literal .node suffix. Distinct IETF special-use namespaces also exist.

SuffixKindMeaningEvidence
.comDelegated public TLDA name may be a brand or alias, not necessarily a commercial company.Standard
.orgDelegated public TLDDoes not prove nonprofit or civil authority.Standard
.netDelegated public TLDDoes not create a distributed network or node.Standard
.siteDelegated public TLDA real public suffix, not proof of a functioning website.Standard

.altSpecial-use non-DNSPossible alternative namespaces with explicit resolvers and collision controls.Standard
home.arpaSpecial-use local DNSFor home-network naming, not publicly reachable site addresses.Standard

No suffix proves domain registration, trademark rights, TLS validity, business/nonprofit status or working software. For private names, avoid inventing *.node public URLs. Prefer correctly scoped names under an owned domain or an explicitly designed, standards-aware alternative resolver.
