03 / Source challenge: evidence versus inference

Three claims an honest auditor should question

“A large political committee spent millions, therefore it bought millions of votes.”

Can that conclusion follow from spending disclosures alone?

No. Campaign spending can support communication and operations. Unlawful vote buying requires specific evidence and applicable legal review. A dollars-per-ballot statistic is descriptive, not a bribery finding. FEC reporting terminology.
“The same allegation appears in 30 AI summaries, so we have 30 independent witnesses.”

What should be verified?

Trace primary documents and genuinely independent source lineages. Thirty models reproducing one assertion may provide one repeated claim and zero independent observations. Account for counterevidence, incomplete context and uncertainty before any public accusation.
“One country has a better corruption index score, so its most recent election must be cleaner.”

Are we comparing the same thing?

No. Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index concerns perceived public-sector corruption. International IDEA describes political-finance laws and institutions; laws do not establish compliance. Neither automatically certifies vote counting, ballot secrecy or a named election. 04 / Public ROI claims, not investor advice or a persuasion playbook

When a powerful donor claims a “return,” ask: return of what?

What is observable?

Publicly reported contributions, committee disbursements, spending categories, original filings and relevant public actions. Each has a different source and reporting period.

What needs independent evidence?

Actual communication reach, changed public decisions, institutional accountability and causal attribution. Expenditure alone does not establish any of these outcomes.

What must not be inferred?

The contents of individual secret ballots; a person's susceptibility to influence; which officeholder is “owned”; or an illegal exchange merely because money and policy activity coexist.

What belongs in a public oversight review?

Applicable disclosure rules, source completeness, official enforcement records, conflicts of interest supported by public evidence, documented corrections and fair responses.

This explains funding accountability to citizens and reviewers. It does not help a donor maximize electoral influence, identify persuadable voters, or target specific political audiences.
