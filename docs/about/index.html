What is CoCivium?

CoCivium is an open research project exploring how people and AI tools can collaborate while keeping evidence, consent, and the ability to challenge decisions visible.

It brings together ideas about shared knowledge, creativity, coordination, and recoverable tools. Some experiments can already be inspected or tried; many larger ambitions remain proposals.

Questions people actually ask

Is CoCivium an app I can install today?

CoCivium is a collection of research ideas and bounded prototypes, not one verified, universally integrated application. The public browser examples should describe their own scope and limitations.

Can it change how my Quest headset works?

Ordinary browser pages cannot replace Meta Quest system controls or make a companion visible inside unrelated apps. An immersive VR application could provide its own objects and controller effects within its own permitted environment; system-wide integration requires separate platform support.

Why so many names beginning with Co?

Names identify related experiments. You do not have to learn the terminology to start. Begin with one visual experience or a small creative task, then follow connections only when useful.

What does CoCivium know about visitors?

This page does not request personal data, location, gaze, camera, microphone, or a login, and does not load third-party analytics scripts. The target website host still processes standard network requests. Other linked services follow their own privacy practices.

Is there a Wikipedia article?

We do not claim one exists. Wikipedia is independently edited, and a new organizational article needs multiple substantial, reliable, secondary sources independent of the organization. A project website or directory listing does not itself satisfy that requirement. The project can publish verified background here while external coverage is reviewed.

Working boundaries: The project aims to be useful, transparent and open to correction. A claim that an experiment is available in source does not prove that it has been deployed, received by a device, or improved someone's life. Specific acceptance tests and privacy controls are needed for those stronger claims.
