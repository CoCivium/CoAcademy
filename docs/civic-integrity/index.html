02 / Election-process transparency · CoProof

What happens between election night and a certified result?

Election-night counts are unofficial. State and local officials complete additional steps under applicable law. The workflow below is educational, and its timing and sequence vary by jurisdiction.

1. Unofficial reports

Early numbers are released and may change as eligible ballots are processed.

2. Canvass

Officials review and reconcile records so eligible votes are properly included.

3. Audits where applicable

Election officials use legally prescribed checks to assess tabulation and procedures.

4. Certification

The responsible authority attests to official results under jurisdictional rules.

EAC: reporting, canvass and certification · EAC: documentation, audits and election management · MIT Election Lab: post-election audits.

What an audit can and cannot establish: Audit methods, samples, legal requirements and reporting vary. This site does not calculate a risk-limiting audit, ingest ballots, evaluate real vote totals or certify any outcome. Apparent discrepancies require the underlying records, jurisdictional context, appropriate qualified reviewers and fair consideration of alternative explanations.
03 / Fair procedures and responsible allegations

Concern about intimidation, coercion, suppression or fraud?

Use an official channel suited to the concern. Do not send allegations or personal details to this experimental site. Do not publicly identify private individuals, share sensitive records or treat a rumor as a finding.

Where and how to reportUSAGov: rights violations, voter intimidation, fraud and appropriate channels U.S. DOJ Civil Rights reportingOfficial civil-rights submission route for potentially relevant violations State or local election authorityVerify jurisdiction-specific election-administration concerns FBI October 2026 voter advisoryOfficial description of categories of federal election crimes
What should be checked before any public allegation?

Identify whether the material is authentic, dated, complete and within the correct jurisdiction; whether the original source can be independently obtained; what alternative explanations exist; whether it concerns a procedural problem, suspected crime, or ordinary counting/certification activity; and which authority is legally responsible. Protect voters, election workers and uninvolved individuals. If there is an immediate threat, contact the appropriate emergency or election authority rather than posting here.

Why not publish an automated fraud scoreboard?

Unverified complaints are not findings, reporting rates are not incidence rates, and machine-generated scores can amplify error or harassment. Publishing individualized accusations or personal voting records would create risks to due process, privacy and public confidence. Independently sourced public documents and fair corrections should come first.

This page does not solicit or store tips. Official reporting routes may have their own privacy policies and legal processes.

04 / CoSkeptic · small evidence experiment

Practice separating an observation from a conclusion.

Three fictional examples. Choose the next responsible step. The responses are educational, not findings about any election.

Example A: a count changes after election night

Someone posts that a jurisdiction's later count differs from its first unofficial report. What follows?

Election-night totals are not final. The canvass and applicable audits may change reported figures. This alone does not establish wrongdoing. Compare official dated results and procedures before interpreting a change. EAC explanation.

Example B: an alarming clip circulates without context

The publisher, date, place and unedited source aren't established. Should it be treated as proof?

No. Verify provenance, original context and legal procedure; separate the clip's observable content from its caption or accusations. Do not identify private people or circulate unsupported allegations. Appropriate election officials can evaluate documented concerns. EAC election documentation and audit guidance.

Example C: someone reports threats at a polling place

They say they are being intimidated or threatened. Where should the issue go?

Direct them toward the relevant election office and official voting-rights reporting routes, including the DOJ Civil Rights Division. For immediate danger, contact emergency services. Do not collect personal incident accounts here. USAGov reporting guidance.
